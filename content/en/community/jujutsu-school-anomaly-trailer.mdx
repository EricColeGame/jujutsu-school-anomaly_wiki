### 1. Calibrate Your Audio Before Stepping Inside
Footstep delays and subtle breathing are primary warning signs. Community reports emphasize playing with high-grade stereo headphones rather than monitor speakers, as directional audio tells you whether a curse is waiting around the corner before your avatar rounds the bend.

### 2. Establish a Strict Visual Scanning Pattern
Do not sprint down the center aisle. Sweep each hallway using a three-point inspection:
- **Ceiling and Lights**: Confirm light fixtures are steady and shadow projections match light sources.
- **Eye-Level Fixtures**: Examine emergency signs, hanging calligraphy scrolls, and window panes.
- **Ground Level**: Look for water puddles, blood splatters, or misplaced shoes outside classroom doors.

| Stage | Inspection Target | What Normal Looks Like | Anomaly Red Flag |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Initial Step** | Hallway Entrance Threshold | Clear wooden floor, intact ceiling beam | Scratches on threshold, flickering lantern |

| **Exit Threshold** | Hallway Exit Marker | Steady green emergency light | Red tint, blinking strobe, missing sign |

### 3. Trust Your Instincts on Micro-Changes
If an area feels subtly wrong—even if you cannot immediately pinpoint why—retreating is statistically safer than pressing forward. Advancing on an overlooked anomaly wipes out your entire progress string, whereas backing away when nothing is wrong only costs you a single loop iteration.
